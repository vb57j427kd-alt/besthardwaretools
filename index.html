More about our OEM / ODM program

From private-label packaging to full product customization, our partner factories handle tooling, color matching, logo printing and pre-shipment inspection. Share your spec sheet or sample - our engineering team replies within 24 hours with a factory-direct quotation and production timeline.

Testing a sample first is the fastest way to lock in quality - we can adjust materials, finishes, packaging and print before you commit to a full container. Your drawings, logos and product specifications stay protected under our IP confidentiality policy, giving importers and brands a safe path to private-label and co-development projects.

Production capacity is pre-validated across our partner lines, with CE-marked output and ISO9001-managed processes so compliance documents, test reports and certificates are ready when your shipment clears customs. We consolidate mixed SKUs - hardware, hand tools, power tools and pneumatic tools - into one container and one invoice, cutting freight cost and simplifying paperwork for multi-category importers and distributors.

Every order is protected from factory floor to your warehouse: export-grade cartons with moisture-resistant packing, container loading supervised at the factory gate, and each batch shipped with the packing list, commercial invoice and compliance certificates your customs broker needs. Need to restock fast? Express freight from major Chinese ports keeps emergency lead times short, while scheduled sea freight protects margins on full containers - so your inventory plan stays predictable whether you order one sample or a 40HQ.

Every OEM batch is inspected against a written sampling plan before shipment, and any defective units found after delivery are replaced or credited on the next order - so branded programs keep moving without quality disputes. Beyond the tool itself we handle the retail details: export cartons printed with your logo, barcode labels, retail-ready packaging and multilingual manuals, so containers land ready for your warehouse, catalog or e-commerce listings. Ask for pre-production samples, photos from the line or a live video inspection before shipment - our export team keeps you in control from tooling approval to final container loading.

From the first drawing to the signed gold sample, one dedicated coordinator keeps your OEM program on schedule, and every shipment leaves with the paperwork your customs broker and end customers expect - commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, test reports and barcode data on request. We keep your tooling, artwork and approved samples on file, so repeat orders are a one-email process with identical output every time. Should anything need attention after arrival, replacement parts are dispatched within days, protecting the brand you have built.

For distributors planning a phased launch, we can begin with a pilot order, lock the approved specification, and scale the same SKU across repeat batches. One project record keeps your artwork, approved sample, packaging dieline, inspection points and shipping plan aligned from quotation through replenishment.

Peak-season planning is where a launch is won or lost, so we book production windows with our partner lines well in advance. Your tooling, mould and packaging lead times are scheduled before the rush rather than queued behind it: share your launch date and target volume and we work backwards from the ship date, reserving capacity, raw materials and inspection slots so branded programs reach the shelf on time.

OEM and ODM projects are built for after-sales continuity rather than a single launch order: wear parts and consumables for the tools in your program can be scheduled alongside the main production run, so replacement stock reaches your warehouse with the same specifications and packaging as the original batch. For retail channels we also prepare market-ready labelling - barcodes, hang tags and importer details printed from the same artwork file - so cartons arrive shelf-ready instead of needing a repacking step on your side.

Not every buyer arrives with finished packaging artwork, so the factory drafts the carton layout, label wording and instruction sheet from whatever you have - a logo file, a photo of the pack you like, or a rough sketch - and sends a digital proof for your sign-off before a printing plate or cutting die is made. The same review is run against your target retail price: where the landed cost has to come down we propose material, finish or packing options that meet it without touching the tooling, and where your market rewards a heavier build we quote that instead. Whatever you approve is filed against your program, so a repeat run is printed from the artwork you signed off rather than being rebuilt from scratch.

When an order includes cordless tools, the lithium battery packs move under transport rules of their own, so the export desk keeps the paperwork each carrier asks for on file and books the shipment with it in hand: the battery test summary, safety data sheet and packing declaration for the exact pack being shipped, plus the carton marks the airline or shipping line requires. That same desk handles the other regulated lines in the range, so a mixed container stays legal to move instead of being split across several bookings. Pallets are built from heat-treated timber to the ISPM 15 standard wherever the destination insists on it, and long sea legs are packed with desiccant so plated hardware and electrical components reach you without surface rust - the two details that most often turn a clean shipment into a claim.

Branding is applied where it survives real use rather than where it photographs well: laser-etched marks on metal tool bodies, pad printing or in-mould labelling on plastic housings, and woven or printed labels on carry cases and textile items, with adhesion checks on the finished part before a production run is released. For a private-label range that runs across categories, one artwork master is applied to hardware, hand tools, power tools and pneumatic lines together, so cartons, labels and product marks carry the same colour reference and typeface - a mixed container then arrives looking like a single brand rather than four suppliers in different dress.

Engineering sign-off runs before volume, not after it: a drawing or a physical sample is reviewed for manufacturability, material and tolerance, the factory builds a first article and returns it with a dimensional report, and the piece you approve is sealed and kept as the golden sample. Every later production run is measured back against that reference, so a shift in material batch or a swap on the line surfaces at inspection instead of at your customer.

Once a private-label run is approved, the same project file is turned into the assets your sales channels need: high-resolution product photography on white, a specification sheet covering dimensions, materials and packing data, and a barcode-ready data block that drops straight into your own listing system. Where your market expects it, the factory also prepares branded retail imagery from the approved sample, so a new SKU goes live on your webstore or marketplace without booking a second photo shoot. Ask for the asset pack together with your quotation - it is prepared alongside the sample, not after the container has sailed.

Freight for a private-label program is quoted on the terms your finance team actually books against - FOB, CIF, DAP or DDP - and the port of loading is chosen per factory cluster, so inland trucking is not paid twice on a mixed branded order. Sailing options are presented with transit time and schedule reliability rather than price alone, and the container slot is held once your deposit clears. Consolidated cargo is received at a nominated warehouse with a free storage window, counted and photographed on arrival, then loaded with the production goods, while the bill of lading is released against your agreed payment terms so the shipping documents reach your broker before the vessel does.

When a compliance team needs evidence before a deposit is committed, the factory profile for each partner line - business licence, social-compliance audit report and product test certificates - can be arranged for review during quotation, with any gap flagged before the order is confirmed rather than after production starts. Buyers who audit their own supply chain can book a factory visit or a video walkthrough of the line, and the findings are logged against the project file so corrective actions are closed before the next run is released - which is what keeps a private-label program defensible in markets where retail buyers screen their suppliers.

A private-label quotation is broken down so the one-time setup stays visible instead of being buried in the piece price: printing plates, cutting dies, logo screens and the first proof are quoted as separate lines, which means a repeat order is priced on production alone rather than paying setup a second time. Where that setup is too heavy for an opening order, the factory proposes a lower-entry route - a stock body in a standard colour finished with a custom label or printed carton - and the fully branded build is scheduled once volume can carry the tooling. Both versions are quoted side by side with their minimum run and the volume at which the branded version becomes the cheaper option, so a distributor can open the program on a small run and still plan the step up.

For a private-label program shipping into regulated retail channels, the artwork file is built to carry what the destination market checks at the border rather than the logo alone: barcode and carton marks, the importer or responsible-person details the regulation requires, the batch or lot code, safety and age warnings in the destination language, and the model designation printed where customs and marketplace compliance teams expect to find it. The factory returns the pack proof with those fields marked up, and a second proof is issued once your side confirms the wording - so a container is not held at the port, and a listing is not rejected, over a label that was approved as a picture instead of as a compliance document.

When a program needs its own mould, cutting die or printing plate, the quotation states who owns that tooling and what it costs to move it: the tool is held against your project reference, the setup is quoted as a one-off line, and if production ever has to move the tool is released or bought out rather than left behind at the factory. A distributor who funds a developed SKU can also request a territory term - a market or channel in which that exact branded item is not quoted to another buyer - reviewed against the volume committed at each renewal, while the standard catalogue lines stay open to every buyer so the rest of the range never depends on a single account.

Once a private-label SKU has been released, every change to it passes through a written change notice instead of a quiet substitution on the line: a revised material, motor, finish or packing method is submitted with the reason for the change, the affected batches and the date it would take effect, and production only moves once your side signs the notice and a new first article has been measured back against the sealed golden sample. The same record flags whether the change touches anything your market regulates - a battery cell, a plug configuration, a coating or a printed warning - so the matching test report or declaration is updated and re-issued alongside it rather than being left at the previous revision. Remaining stock of the earlier build is identified by lot and either shipped against the terms already agreed or held back, so a repeat order never arrives as a mixed consignment of two builds under one item number.

Packaging for an EU or UK program is treated as a regulated material stream rather than a printed box: each pack is built from a documented bill of materials - board grade with its FSC chain-of-custody reference, the plastic content of bags, clips and windows, and the ink or coating applied to the printed surface - and that record is issued with the artwork proof, so your compliance team files recyclability and extended producer responsibility data against the exact pack it receives instead of a generic specification. Where a retail buyer sets a reduction target, single-material alternatives are quoted beside the original: a printed carton carrying the instructions instead of a separate leaflet, a paper hang tag in place of a plastic hanger, and a moulded pulp tray replacing a thermoformed insert, each with its weight change per unit and the effect on carton cube, so the saving is weighed against freight before a decision is made. Country-specific EPR registration numbers and the importer details the rule names are printed where the regulation requires them, and a revised pack only enters production once its declaration has been re-issued - so a shipment does not arrive compliant on the tool and non-compliant on the packaging.
